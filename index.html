First-aid guides

Simple steps to take while getting professional help.

Emergency learning
Urgent
🩸

Severe bleeding

  1. Check the scene is safe. Use gloves or a clean barrier if available.
  2. Press firmly and continuously on the wound with a clean dressing or cloth.
  3. If blood soaks through, add more material without removing the first layer.
  4. Call an ambulance for heavy or uncontrolled bleeding.
More safety adviceFor life-threatening limb bleeding, use a commercial tourniquet only if available and you know how, or follow dispatcher instructions.
Urgent
🫁

Choking

  • If the person can cough or speak, encourage coughing and watch closely.
  • If an adult or child cannot breathe, speak or cough effectively, call for help and give up to 5 back blows.
  • If not relieved, give up to 5 abdominal thrusts; repeat cycles while getting help.
  • For a baby under 1 year, use 5 back blows then 5 chest thrusts—never abdominal thrusts.
ImportantDo not do blind finger sweeps. If they become unresponsive, call emergency services and start CPR if you can.
First aid
🔥

Burns and scalds

  1. Move away from the heat source if safe.
  2. Cool under cool running water for 20 minutes.
  3. Remove jewellery or clothing near the burn unless stuck to skin.
  4. Cover loosely with a clean, non-fluffy dressing.
Never do thisDo not use ice, butter, toothpaste or oil. Get urgent care for large/deep burns and chemical or electrical burns, or burns to the face, hands, genitals or major joints.
Urgent
❤️

Possible heart attack

  • Warning signs can include chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, or pain in an arm, back, neck or jaw.
  • Call an ambulance immediately. Do not let the person drive.
  • Help them rest in a comfortable position and stay with them.
  • If they collapse and are not breathing normally, call for help and start CPR if trained; use an AED if available.
Urgent
🧠

Recognise a stroke: FAST

  • Face: one side droops when smiling.
  • Arms: one arm is weak or drifts down.
  • Speech: speech is slurred or unusual.
  • Time: call an ambulance immediately and note when symptoms began.

Do not give food, drink or medicine unless a clinician directs you.

Urgent
🆘

Unconscious person

  1. Check safety, responsiveness and breathing; call for help.
  2. If unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR and follow dispatcher instructions.
  3. If unconscious but breathing normally, place on their side unless a serious spinal injury is suspected; monitor breathing.
CPR basicsHands-on training is recommended. For an adult, compress the centre of the chest hard and fast at 100–120 compressions per minute. Use an AED if available and follow its prompts.
Urgent
☠️

Poisoning or chemicals

  • Protect yourself from the substance; do not touch or taste it.
  • Call emergency medical help and follow instructions.
  • Do not make the person vomit or give home remedies unless a medical professional directs you.
  • Keep the container or label for medical staff, if safe.
Road safety
🚗

Road traffic crash

  • Stop somewhere safe, away from moving traffic.
  • Call emergency services and give the location and number of injured people.
  • Do not move injured people unless there is immediate danger.
  • Control severe bleeding and keep the person warm while waiting.
Urgent
🌊

Drowning and water emergencies

  • Do not enter the water unless trained and it is safe. Reach or throw flotation from shore.
  • Call for help; remove the person from water only when safe.
  • If they are not breathing normally, start CPR and follow emergency instructions.
  • Breathing trouble after rescue needs urgent medical care.
Urgent
🐍

Snakebite

  • Move away from the snake; do not try to catch or kill it.
  • Keep the person calm and still; immobilise the bitten limb.
  • Get urgent medical care. Remove rings or tight items before swelling.
  • Do not cut, suck, ice, burn or tightly tie the bite. Avoid traditional remedies.
First aid
🧍

Seizure / fits

  • Move dangerous objects away and cushion the head.
  • Do not restrain the person or put anything in their mouth.
  • Time the seizure. When movements stop, check breathing.
  • Call emergency services if it lasts 5 minutes, repeats, is a first seizure, occurs in water, or breathing/recovery is difficult.
Heat safety
☀️

Heat illness

  • Move to shade or a cool place and loosen excess clothing.
  • Cool the skin with water, wet cloths and fanning.
  • If confused, collapsing or very hot, treat as a medical emergency.
  • Do not give drinks to someone confused or unconscious.

Health and disease information

Know warning signs, reduce risk and seek care early.

🦟

Malaria: prevention and warning signs

Malaria can become severe quickly. Fever, chills, headache and body aches may occur, but symptoms alone cannot confirm it.

  • Seek prompt testing and treatment for suspected malaria, especially for children and pregnant people.
  • Use insecticide-treated nets and follow local mosquito-control advice.
  • Confusion, seizures, difficulty breathing, severe weakness or inability to drink require emergency care.

Do not self-prescribe antimalarial medicines.

💧

Diarrhoea and dehydration

  • Use correctly prepared oral rehydration solution (ORS) from a packet and safe water, following the packet instructions exactly.
  • Continue breastfeeding and offer suitable food as tolerated.
  • Seek urgent care for blood in stool, inability to drink, very little urine, marked sleepiness or worsening symptoms.

Do not guess the amount of water when mixing ORS powder.

🫁

Tuberculosis (TB)

A cough lasting two weeks or more, chest pain, fever, night sweats or unexplained weight loss should be checked by a health professional.

  • Visit a health facility for screening and testing.
  • Cover coughs, improve ventilation and follow health-worker advice.
  • TB is treatable. Take prescribed treatment exactly as directed.
🤝

HIV: testing, prevention and care

  • HIV testing is the only way to know your status.
  • Condoms, sterile injecting equipment and prescribed PrEP can reduce risk.
  • Effective antiretroviral treatment helps people live long, healthy lives.
  • Use confidential, qualified health services and avoid stigma.

Ask a local health facility about current testing, PrEP and treatment services.

🩺

High blood pressure

High blood pressure often has no obvious symptoms, so measurement matters.

  • Get blood pressure checked at a health facility or suitable clinic.
  • Reduce excess salt, stay active, avoid tobacco and follow clinical advice.
  • Do not stop prescribed medicine just because you feel well.
🤰

Pregnancy: danger signs

Seek urgent maternity care for heavy bleeding, severe abdominal pain, severe headache or vision changes, difficulty breathing, seizures, or reduced fetal movement when movements are usually felt.

  • Attend antenatal visits and follow advice from skilled health workers.
  • Plan where to deliver and how to get there day or night.
🩸

Diabetes awareness

Frequent urination, unusual thirst, unexplained weight loss and fatigue can be warning signs. A test is needed for diagnosis.

  • Seek a health assessment if symptoms appear.
  • Follow prescribed treatment and keep follow-up appointments.
  • Vomiting, deep/rapid breathing, confusion or unconsciousness require emergency care.
🧼

Clean water and hygiene

  • Use safe water; if uncertain, boil it and store it in a clean, covered container.
  • Wash hands with soap after toilet use and before preparing or eating food.
  • Keep food covered and clean shared surfaces.
  • Severe watery diarrhoea can cause dangerous dehydration—seek urgent care.
💉

Vaccination

Vaccines protect individuals and communities from serious infections.

  • Follow Zambia's current immunisation schedule and health-worker advice.
  • Keep the child's health/immunisation card and attend scheduled visits.
  • Ask a qualified provider about missed doses rather than restarting or guessing.

Disaster and survival guides

Prepare before floods, fires, storms or other disruptions.

🌧️

Flood readiness

  • Move to higher ground when warned by authorities.
  • Never walk or drive through floodwater; it may be deeper or faster than it looks.
  • Keep documents in a waterproof bag and take essential medicines if evacuating.
  • Avoid fallen power lines and report them from a safe distance.
🧯

Home fire safety

  • Plan two exits and an outdoor meeting place.
  • Get out, stay out and call for help. Do not return for belongings.
  • Never use charcoal braziers indoors or in poorly ventilated rooms; carbon monoxide can kill.
  • Keep fuel, matches and flammable liquids away from heat and children.
⚡

Lightning and storms

  • Go inside a substantial building or enclosed hard-topped vehicle.
  • Avoid open fields, isolated trees, water and metal fences.
  • Wait at least 30 minutes after the last thunder before returning outdoors.
🎒

Emergency kit

  • Safe drinking water and non-perishable food.
  • Torch, spare batteries or charged power bank.
  • Essential medicines, first-aid supplies and hygiene items.
  • Copies of key documents, emergency contacts and some cash stored safely.
☠️

Carbon monoxide danger

This gas has no colour or smell and can kill. Risk sources include charcoal, generators and fuel-burning devices indoors.

  • Never operate a generator or charcoal brazier inside a house, room or enclosed space.
  • If exposure is suspected, get outside to fresh air if safe and call emergency help.
  • Do not re-enter until authorities say it is safe.
🚙

Roadside breakdown

  • Move off the road if possible and switch on hazard lights.
  • Stay away from traffic; do not stand behind or in front of the vehicle.
  • Share your location with a trusted contact and contact roadside assistance.
  • Do not accept risky help or walk along an unsafe road at night.

Mental health and emotional support

Support can start with listening, safety and connecting to professional care.

💚

Supporting someone in distress

  • Listen calmly and take their feelings seriously.
  • Ask what support would help; avoid judgement and promises you cannot keep.
  • Encourage contact with a trusted health worker or mental health professional.
  • Small steps such as sleep routines, social connection and manageable daily activities may help alongside professional care.
🫶

If someone may harm themselves

  • If safe, stay with them and listen without arguing or shaming them.
  • Ask directly whether they are thinking about suicide; asking does not put the idea in their head.
  • Help connect them with emergency services, a health professional or trusted person now.
  • Reduce access to immediate dangers only if you can do so safely.

If danger is immediate, call emergency services or go to the nearest emergency department. This website does not monitor messages or provide crisis response.
